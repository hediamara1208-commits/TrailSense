# Fact-check of the numbers we quote (D10)

Status: ✅ verified against the source, ⚠️ correct but needs care when phrased.

| Claim | Status | Source and exact wording |
|---|---|---|
| Contrails ≈ 57 % of aviation's net effective radiative forcing | ✅ | Lee et al., *Atmos. Environ.* 244, 117834 (2021): contrail cirrus ERF 57.4 mW m⁻² out of 100.9 mW m⁻² net aviation ERF (2018). Say "more than half", not "57 % of aviation's emissions". |
| −54 % contrails on trial flights | ✅ ⚠️ | Google blog, 8 Aug 2023: American Airlines pilots "flew 70 test flights over six months" and "were able to reduce contrails by 54%"; those flights "burned 2% additional fuel". Not a randomised trial (no control group). Fleet-wide "as low as 0.3%" fuel is an **estimate**. |
| −11.6 % intent-to-treat (2026 trial) | ✅ | Sankar et al., *Efficacy of Scalable Airline-led Contrail Avoidance*, arXiv:2603.06909 (Mar 2026), randomised, American Airlines transatlantic flights, Jan-May 2025: "an 11.6% reduction in contrail formation rate for the 1232 flights marked as eligible" (p = 0.011). Only **112** flights flew the plan; those had **62.0 %** fewer contrails (p < 0.001). "No statistically significant difference in fuel usage was observed." |
| 2 % of flights → 80 % of contrail warming | ✅ ⚠️ | Teoh et al., *Environ. Sci. Technol.* 54, 2941 (2020), Japanese airspace: ~2.2 % of flights caused 80 % of the contrail energy forcing. Diverting **1.7 %** of flights cut it by **59.3 %** for **+0.014 %** fuel. Regional, model-based study. |
| 50 / 15 kg CO2e per km (night / day) | ✅ ⚠️ | Borella et al., *ACP* 24, 9401 (2024): mean EF 9.6 × 10¹⁰ J km⁻¹ (verified in Sect. 3), efficacy 0.37, AGWP100 of CO₂ → ≈ 24 kg CO2e/km on average. The night/day split (50/15) is **our** choice, shown with a ÷3 to ×3 range. See `docs/hypotheses.md`. |

## Suggested README wording
> Avoiding them works on the flights that follow the plan (−54 % in a 2023 Google / American Airlines test, −62 % in a 2025 randomised trial),
> but adoption is the bottleneck: only 112 of ~1,200 eligible flights flew the avoidance plan, so the whole group saw −11.6 %
> (Sankar et al., 2026). That is exactly the dispatcher's problem TrailSense addresses.
